How it works

Two branches, one print

A scan goes down two paths at once. Faces are found, aligned and rebuilt one by one; the whole frame is upscaled and cleaned. They meet again when each face is pasted back, then the tones are levelled.

1Scan→ 2Remove grain→ 3Find faces→ 4Rebuild faces→ 5Upscale frame→ 6Paste & level→ 7Print

Faces, from a learned prior

Each face is cut to a 512-pixel template and rebuilt by GFPGAN, a generator trained on real faces. Eyes, skin and hair come back sharp while the identity is held in place.

GFPGAN v1.4RetinaFace

The whole frame, upscaled

Real-ESRGAN enlarges the frame four times and clears scan and compression artefacts on the way. Clothes, backgrounds and edges all gain detail, not just the faces.

Your photos stay yours

Heirloom processes scans on the machine it runs on. Originals are read, never modified; every result is written as a new file beside them. No image is sent to a third-party service.

Restored faces are a reconstruction: GFPGAN regenerates skin and eye detail from a learned prior, so the result is a faithful likeness rather than a recovered negative. The face-rebuild control lets you keep more of the original texture.

Models: GFPGAN v1.4, Real-ESRGAN x4plus, RetinaFace (facexlib), ParseNet. Research use of the published models under their respective licences.
